The register is a **contractual-arrangement** record, not a vendor list. The
unit is the arrangement; one provider under three contracts is three rows, and
the same provider reached through an intermediary is a chain that must be
traceable end to end.

## What each arrangement must carry

- the provider's legal identity and identifier (LEI where held);
- the ICT service supplied, in the function's own terms;
- **whether it supports a critical or important function** — the determination
  that drives every other duty;
- start date, end date and notice period;
- the country where the service is performed and where data is stored;
- subcontracting that is material to the function, named rather than permitted
  in the abstract;
- exit arrangements and the tested exit plan where the function is CIF.

## Judging critical or important

Ask what fails if the arrangement stops tomorrow. It is CIF when the disruption
would materially impair financial performance, the soundness or continuity of
services, or compliance with authorisation conditions. Record the reasoning, not
only the verdict — the reasoning is what a supervisor tests.

A CIF determination raises the contractual floor (Article 30(3)): full service
descriptions, locations, data-protection terms, access/inspection/audit rights,
exit strategies, and incident-assistance duties.

## Before a submission

Reject the register when an arrangement lacks a CIF determination, when a CIF
arrangement has no exit plan, when subcontracting is described only as permitted,
or when a provider appears under inconsistent identifiers across rows — the last
reads as several providers and understates concentration.
